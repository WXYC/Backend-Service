# Ops: LML-hitting cron spacing policy (BS#1665)

Policy for spacing the crons that hit library-metadata-lookup (LML) over HTTP, so the cron stack can't re-trip LML's Discogs saturation breaker (LML#755) the way it did on 2026-07-11 06:00 UTC — two heavy-drain crons fired at the same `HH:MM`, tripping the breaker into a 4-day continuous shed storm (32,520 shed events, LIBRARY-METADATA-LOOKUP-1Q). See WXYC/library-metadata-lookup#803 for the postmortem and BS#1665 for the fix this doc records.

The breaker trips on LML's Discogs API work, driven by LML's HTTP endpoints — not by DB reads. Scheduling policy only applies to LML-over-HTTP crons.

## Three cron shapes

**1. Heavy-drain crons** — unbounded or large-cohort LML sweeps (`bulkLookupMetadata`, per-row lookups over a large backlog).

- **Hard invariant:** no two heavy-drain crons share the same `HH:MM`. This is the literal 2026-07-11 failure mode — two heavy drains firing simultaneously, not the existence of heavy drains per se.
- **Recommended margin:** ≥60 min apart, to reduce the odds that a _slow_ run of one overlaps the _start_ of another.
- Runtime overlap itself (a run still alive when the next day's stack fires) is **not** a scheduling problem — a 23-hour run overlaps from any start time. That's a runtime-liveness concern, folded into BS#1201 (cron heartbeats + max-runtime guard), not this policy.

**2. Light-touch crons** — bounded small-cohort LML calls (the concerts pipeline, `artist-search-alias-consumer`), each already gated by the job's own limiter. Exempt from the ≥60 min margin; total LML call volume per run is a handful. May cluster.

**3. DB-only jobs** — read LML's cache tables directly (e.g. `entity.identity`) over a direct Postgres connection, never call LML's HTTP API, and cannot trip the breaker. Out of scope for this policy entirely. Listed explicitly so a future author doesn't try to "space" them.

### BS#2218: `flowsheet-no-match-recheck` run duration widened

That job now sends `budgetMs: null` — no `X-Caller-Budget-Ms` at all — so a genuinely cold candidate runs LML's full cascade (bounded by LML's own 25s `LML_SEARCH_HARD_TIMEOUT_MS`) instead of fast-degrading near 4s. Its class stays 5; only the budget header is suppressed, and only for this caller.

Worst case per run widens from ~10 min (200 rows at `BACKFILL_LML_RATE_PER_MIN=20`) to ~83 min (200 rows × LML's 25s hard cap at `BACKFILL_LML_MAX_CONCURRENT=1`), and the true ceiling is higher still: the binding limit is this job's own client-side abort, `FLOWSHEET_NO_MATCH_RECHECK_LML_PER_CALL_TIMEOUT_MS` (35s), so a run in which every call rides a wedged connection is ~117 min — plus up to `LIVE_ACTIVITY_MAX_PAUSE_MS` (30 min default) of cooperative pause on top. Budget against ~2.5h, not ~1.5h, when slotting the next cron. That is still inside the 6h cadence and does not create new overlap with `rotation-release-id-backfill` — that job's next start is 6h out, not 30 min out — but it is a materially different load shape against the shared Discogs rate bucket than the pre-fix job. Per BS#2218, if that load proves too high the levers are `FLOWSHEET_NO_MATCH_RECHECK_BATCH_SIZE` and run frequency, **not** re-arming the header: the header is what self-locked the job in the first place.

BS#2218 also made this the first job to write `cronjob_runs.cursor_position` (migration 0152). That column is **opaque and job-private**: its meaning is defined entirely by the owning job's module, and a second job opting in may store something with completely different semantics under its own `job_name` row. Do not read another job's `cursor_position` or assume a shared interpretation. `last_run` remains the shared, fleet-wide liveness field, and this job stamps it on every completed run.

## The hourly safety net (BS#895)

`flowsheet-metadata-backfill` runs as an hourly `10 * * * *` recovery sweep, per `package.json`'s `cron-schedule`. It is **exempt from slot-exclusivity by construction** — governed by the static LML gate (`BACKFILL_LML_MAX_CONCURRENT=1`, `BACKFILL_LML_RATE_PER_MIN=20`) + cooperative pause, not by its slot. Its `:10` offset was chosen because it was clear of every current cron and of the non-LML ETL trio (`:00` was taken by `rotation-lml-identity-backfill` after BS#1665 plus the `*/30` ETL trio; `:30` by `rotation-artist-backfill` plus the same trio). That trio is now a pair (see "Excluded / DB-only") — the `:10` slot is still correct, just less contended than when it was picked. Its own workload is bounded by two knobs (`BACKFILL_GRACE_MINUTES`, default 15 — gives the CDC consumer first crack at a fresh row; `BACKFILL_RECOVERY_WINDOW_HOURS`, default 6 — hard age ceiling that excludes the undrained historical `pending` backlog #1011 left behind (≈163k rows as of 2026-08-26, down from ~748k after the 2026-08-01→08-12 catch-up drain (BS#2279))), not by this policy's heavy-drain/light-touch classification.

## Grandfathered margin gap: 04:30 / 04:45

`rotation-artist-backfill` (04:30) and `catalog-popularity-freetext-resolve` (04:45) are two heavy drains only 15 min apart — they satisfy the hard invariant (distinct `HH:MM`) but sit below the ≥60 min recommended margin. Deliberately grandfathered rather than re-laid, because:

- They predate the 2026-07-11 incident and neither independently tripped the breaker — the trip required two heavy drains firing at the _same instant_.
- Each is paced by its own static LML limiter.
- Genuine runtime-overlap protection is BS#1201's job, not the schedule's.

Promoting them to full ≥60 min compliance later is a trivial one-line follow-up, not silent debt.

## Margin ceiling: `flowsheet-no-match-recheck` / `rotation-release-id-backfill` (30 min, not promotable)

`flowsheet-no-match-recheck` (`47 */6 * * *`) and `rotation-release-id-backfill` (`17 */6 * * *`) are two heavy drains sharing the same `*/6`-hour anchor grid (00/06/12/18), 30 minutes apart (BS#2179 review LOW finding) — below the ≥60 min recommended margin but, unlike the 04:30/04:45 pair above, **not a promotable gap**: both crons recur every 6 hours from the same top-of-hour anchor, so the achievable separation between any two fixed minutes on that shared grid is capped at 30 min (two points on a 60-minute circle are farthest apart, at most, on opposite sides — `|47 − 17| = 30`, and `60 − 30 = 30` the other way round). Moving `flowsheet-no-match-recheck` to any other minute only trades which side of `:17` it sits on, never widens the gap past 30. Reaching ≥60 min would require moving one job off the shared `*/6` anchor entirely (a different, non-6-hourly cadence, or an hour-offset anchor like `1,7,13,19`) — a cadence-shape change out of scope for a spacing fix, and not justified here: both crons are TTL-gated with their own dedicated per-job limiter (`BACKFILL_LML_MAX_CONCURRENT=1` / `BACKFILL_LML_RATE_PER_MIN=20`-shaped knobs), satisfy the hard invariant (distinct `HH:MM`), and a slow run of one 30 minutes before the other is the same runtime-overlap concern BS#1201 owns, not this policy. Documented rather than silently accepted, same as the 04:30/04:45 pair.

## Adding a new LML-hitting cron

1. Classify it: heavy-drain, light-touch, or DB-only.
2. Heavy-drain: pick an `HH:MM` no other heavy-drain cron holds, ideally ≥60 min from the nearest one. Update the slot table above.
3. Light-touch: any minute is fine, but prefer clustering near the existing concerts-pipeline block (05:35–06:05) for readability.
4. DB-only: no constraint from this policy.
5. If the job needs an ops-tunable cadence override, do **not** add it to the shared `BACKFILL_CRON_SCHEDULE` allowlist in `scripts/resolve-cron-schedule.sh` unless its cadence story is genuinely the same as `flowsheet-metadata-backfill`'s — see the BS#1665 postmortem for why a shared override var is a latent re-collision route.

## Cron liveness (BS#2064)

Scheduling is not the only thing a cron can get wrong. A job whose healthy run is a no-op — anti-joined on an empty cohort, nothing to write, `candidates: 0` in the log — is byte-identical to a job that **stopped running**. A `try`/`catch` + `captureError` covers "the job ran and threw"; it cannot see the crontab entry dropped by a deploy or a hand edit, the image failing to pull, docker wedged, or the host rebooting. That gap is what BS#2064 closed for `legacy-linkage-resolve`, the first cron in the fleet to carry a liveness signal.

**The recipe, for any cron whose idle run is a no-op:**

1. **Sentry cron monitor.** Wrap the run in `Sentry.withMonitor(JOB_NAME, callback, MONITOR_CONFIG)` — `@sentry/node` ≥ 7. Check yours before assuming: as of BS#2071, 31 of the 44 packages under `jobs/` depend on it, not all. Of the 13 that don't: 10 are one-shots that never needed it, `rotation-release-id-pollution-check` is a scheduled Python job with no Node dependency at all, and the remaining two — `artist-identity-etl` (`0 * * * *`) and `library-etl` (`*/30 * * * *`) — are scheduled Node crons doing real fan-out work that simply haven't had the recipe applied yet; don't read the "handful of one-shots" framing as covering them. Sentry upserts the monitor from the config on the first check-in (nothing to click in the UI) and alerts on a **missed** check-in, which is the actual failure mode. Set `schedule` to the job's `package.json` `cron-schedule` (`{ type: 'crontab', value }`), a `checkinMargin` under one cadence, and a `maxRuntime` under one cadence so a wedged run is flagged before the next fires. Set `timezone: 'Etc/UTC'` — `deploy-base.yml` installs the crontab string verbatim with no TZ line, so the host's own clock is what runs it. That line is a **no-op for an interval expression** like `*/30` (every 30th minute is the same instant in any zone) but **load-bearing for a wall-clock cron** (`0 9 * * *`, `07 15 * * *`) — confirm the host's actual TZ before pasting the line on faith; don't assume it's always `Etc/UTC` just because this recipe's first user was. Pin `CRON_SCHEDULE` against `package.json` with a unit test — tsup does not bundle `package.json` into `dist/`, so the value has to be duplicated in code and can drift. `withMonitor` re-throws, so the existing `captureError` path is unchanged. Requires `SENTRY_DSN` in the EC2 `.env`; without a DSN the SDK no-ops and the signal silently vanishes.
2. **`cronjob_runs` heartbeat** via the shared `updateLastRun`, written inside the monitored callback so the check-in reports `ok` only when the whole unit of work committed. **A heartbeat nothing reads is not a signal** — have the next run read it back and log/warn on the elapsed gap. Set the gap threshold several cadences out, so it stays a Sentry-independent backstop rather than a second copy of the check-in alert.
3. **An outcome check**, if the job has one that is cheap and provably quiet on a healthy run. It catches the case the first two miss: a job that runs, checks in green, and silently stops doing its work. `legacy-linkage-resolve` measures its cohort count and its UPDATE inside a single data-modifying CTE — one statement, one snapshot — and warns when the count of rows actually updated falls short of the cohort it saw. Comparing across two separate statements (a COUNT, then an UPDATE) looks equivalent but isn't: each statement takes its own Postgres snapshot, so a concurrent writer landing in the gap between them reads as a false positive in one direction or the other, on a run where nothing was actually wrong (BS#2071).

**Cost.** Every paid Sentry plan includes exactly **one** cron monitor; additional monitors are PAYG-only at **$0.78/monitor/month**. WXYC is on Team ($29/mo, $100 PAYG cap) — so the first monitor is free and each subsequent one is a real, if small, line item. Check-ins bill under a **separate quota category from errors**, which is the main argument for (1): a missed-check-in alert survives an error-quota exhaustion of the kind that took every WXYC project dark on 2026-06-03 (BS#1291). Before fanning the recipe across all of `jobs/`, price it: `jobs/` holds **44** job directories, of which **22** resolve to a scheduled cron (the other 22 are `job-type: one-shot` and never touch the crontab — see `.github/workflows/deploy-base.yml`'s `job-type` branch). `legacy-linkage-resolve`'s monitor is the plan's one free slot, so fanning the recipe out to the remaining 21 scheduled crons is 21 × $0.78 ≈ **$16/mo** of PAYG. Prefer monitoring the crons whose silence is expensive.

**Relationship to BS#1201.** #1201 proposes the CloudWatch-side version: the cron _wrapper_ emits `WXYC/Cron/HeartbeatSuccess` after a successful `docker run`, with per-cron alarms on missing datapoints. That catches a container that never starts (this recipe's signal (1) also does, since no process means no check-in) and does not depend on Sentry. The two are complementary; nothing here forecloses #1201. If a CloudWatch route is added, follow the org metric/alarm conventions — a plain-form alarm needs a dimensionless companion series emitted in the same `PutMetricData` call, or it sits at "no data" forever (the wxyc-canary#13 post-mortem).
