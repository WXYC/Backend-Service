# 0006 — Reviews are many-per-release, one accepted by a music director as the record's review, consent-gated per surface

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-10-02; amended to the station's decisions of 2026-10-04. Mirrors the canonical cross-repo ADR 0005. This file supersedes the one-per-album, author-owned, MD-curated-queue model that an earlier version of this ADR described; that model was superseded before any of it was built. The 2026-10-04 amendment replaced the print-lock paragraph, which also limited printing to intake items, and edited the notices, gate and consent paragraphs in place; the text it replaced survives only in this file's git history.

Canonical source: [`WXYC/wxyc-dj-ios/docs/cross-repo-adrs.md` ADR 0005](https://github.com/WXYC/wxyc-dj-ios/blob/main/docs/cross-repo-adrs.md#adr-0005--reviews-are-many-per-release-one-accepted-by-a-music-director-as-the-records-review-consent-gated-per-surface).

A review is about an _intake item_ (a physical copy the station holds, logged by a music director) or an existing library release, never a record the station does not hold. A release can have many reviews. Its content is what the station's printed slip carries: buzzwords, a paragraph about the artist, the review itself, recommended tracks, and an FCC line, all free text, plus the publishing consent described below. There is no rating, no headline, no per-track polarity, and no curated tag vocabulary.

Intake items wait on the review shelf. A music director logs each one onto the review shelf, or requests a named DJ for it, which holds the record in the office for that DJ. A DJ claims an item by checking it out, which means physically taking the record; a requested DJ accepts (a checkout) or passes, and a pass notifies the music directors. A request lapses back to the review shelf after 7 days. A checkout never lapses, though after 14 days it is flagged overdue to the music directors. The holder or a music director can release a checked-out item back to the review shelf at any time. A music director can delete an item at any point before filing it, and its reviews are deleted with it.

Account holders write their own reviews. A DJ can start a review of an intake item only while holding it, because reviewing needs the physical record, and can review any release already in the library; trainees may review library releases but are steered to the review shelf. A music director records reviews on behalf of anyone else. The handwritten route is open to everyone: the review is written on paper and stays on the sleeve, and a music director records it in the app with all of its text optional and nothing printed. `author` is text with an optional account link (`author_user_id`), because most historical reviewers never had a Backend-Service account.

Drafts are private to their author and to whoever recorded them (`recorded_by_user_id`); music directors do not see other people's drafts. Notices are email only; there is no in-app inbox, and for music directors "in the app" means their queue of records with reviews waiting. The music directors are emailed when a review of an intake item is submitted, when a DJ passes an item, when an FCC note is reported, and when an edit changes the FCC line of a printed review. A DJ is emailed when a music director edits their review or records one in their name. This is the epic's Notifications list (WXYC/Backend-Service#2791); the slice that sends the FCC-report email is WXYC/Backend-Service#2863. Submitting a review of a library release notifies nobody.

A music director accepts the record's review. A record can have many reviews. Submitting one never moves the intake item; it notifies the music directors. The item becomes `reviewed` only when a music director accepts a specific review, and that review is the one the slip prints. A music director can accept a different review at any time, before or after filing, which is how a review is replaced. A review a music director records themselves (on someone's behalf, or handwritten) is accepted by default, with an opt-out, and can be recorded at any stage of a record's life, including after filing. There is no reject or send-back state: an unwanted review is simply not accepted, or is deleted. The music director is shown whether a waiting review comes from the DJ who currently has the record (checked out to them, or requested of them), and a reviewed record keeps its holder until the DJ returns it or it is filed. A pending request is withdrawn when a music director accepts a review for the record; the DJ is not told, and may still review the record from the review shelf.

There is no print lock. An author edits their own review at any time, printed or not. Music directors may edit the text of any review, and the author is told by email. Every version of a submitted review is kept and readable by anyone who can read the review; the app shows the current text and points to the printed version. Drafts are not versioned, and deleting a review deletes its history. Publishing consent is set only by the review's author account (see the consent paragraph below); when an on-behalf review is linked to a DJ's account, that DJ is its author, may edit it, and alone sets its consent.

Every release has a slip. The slip can be printed as soon as a review is accepted; filing first is not required. Every print is logged: which review, which version, who, and when. A music director can also print any typed, submitted review of any release already in the library, including releases catalogued before the cutover (`POST /library/{id}/print`), so a cover's review can be replaced. A citation means no new review has to be written, not that nothing is printed: a record that cites another release takes its cover review from it, a music director choosing one of the cited release's typed reviews for the record, and it prints like any other. Changing or removing the citation before filing takes that review off the record. If the cited release is later deleted from the catalog, the record keeps its own complete copy of that review (decision 35 in the epic, WXYC/Backend-Service#2791, added 2026-10-05 and tracked in WXYC/Backend-Service#2875). A review that exists only in the Google Form archive, or only on paper, is copied from the form review, or typed in by a music director, onto the record as a review the music director recorded, and that typed copy is the one chosen and printed like any other; the archive stays a separate table. This is not the handwritten route above: a review recorded as handwritten stays on the sleeve as the record's slip, and nothing is printed for it.

An author cannot delete a review that is in use (accepted for a record, or the latest one printed for a copy). A music director can delete any review, except that the accepted review of a filed record cannot be deleted until another is accepted, unless the record was filed on a citation. Deleting the accepted review of a record not yet filed returns it to its holder, or to the review shelf. Deleting a review deletes its history.

FCC notes can also sit on the record, separate from a review's own FCC line. Any DJ can report an FCC note against a library release or a review-shelf item. It shows to all DJs at once as reported and not yet confirmed; music directors confirm or remove it, and the DJ who reported it may remove their own note while it is still unconfirmed. Removing a note deletes it; there is no removed state. A review's own FCC line always prints; of the record's notes, only confirmed ones print on the slip.

A review carries no routing: no outcome and no recommendation. The music director alone decides whether a release goes to rotation or straight to the shelf, and assigns its call number when filing it, before it is shelved; filing creates the library row, and the rotation entry if there is one, in one transaction. The librarian finalizes the release when shelving it, confirming or changing the call number.

After a cutover date, a hard gate applies: every release entering the library or rotation needs an accepted review, which includes a handwritten one recorded by a music director, or a citation. Releases catalogued before the cutover are deemed reviewed, since their physical reviews are already taped to their covers. A citation names either a release that has a submitted review or is deemed reviewed (for a second format or a replacement copy), or a form-archive submission dated before the form closed (including a record reviewed on the form before the cutover, which cites its own submission). A typed-text rotation record from before the cutover may still change bins after it, and keeps its right to be imported into the library by the librarian. The form and the app overlap through the rest of fall 2026; the cutover, and the form's close, is one day at the start of the spring 2027 semester. In this repository the gate is `REVIEW_GATE_CUTOVER_DATE` (unset means off; a set value that is not a real `YYYY-MM-DD` date is reported and treated as on; the gate is on from the cutover date itself, and a stored date equal to it counts as before it, per `docs/env-vars.md`, whose helper `apps/backend/utils/review-gate-cutover.ts` is the one reader), enforced inside `insertAlbum` and `addToRotation` through a required `gateBasis` argument (`intake`, `existing_release`, `legacy_import`, `legacy_move`, or `pre_cutover`) that each function verifies against the record it names before it will insert.

Consent is collected per surface: one checkbox each for the website, the WXYC apps, and Instagram, plus one credit choice: DJ name (only if the account has one), real name, or no name. Only a review's author, through their own account, sets these choices; nobody else can, a music director included. A review recorded on someone's behalf starts with nothing ticked, and a review with no linked account has nobody who can set its consent. A review's FCC line is never published, on any surface, and neither are the record's FCC notes. v1 collects this consent and publishes nothing new with it.

Author names may be shown anywhere inside the station: dj-site and the DJ apps. The form's anonymity promise covers only outside the station. Amended 2026-10-08: inside the station people are shown by real name, falling back to `auth_user.name` (the on-air handle, else username) when an account has none. The stored stamps follow it (`reviews.author` for an account holder's own review, `review_revisions.edited_by`, `fcc_notes.reported_by` and `confirmed_by`), through one allow-listed helper, `shared/database/src/staff-name.ts`. The credit choice above is a different thing, the author's publishing preference; and no public surface reads these stamps.

The form archive ([ADR 0011](0011-album-review-submissions-separate-archive.md)) stays a separate table; it is cited, never merged into `reviews`. Three of these decisions reach it: reviewer names may be shown inside the station; an intake item may cite an archive submission dated before the form closed; and a form-era "yes" to sharing counts for all three surfaces, always uncredited.

## Consequences

- Schema deltas: add `intake_items` and `intake_item_passes`; extend `reviews` to many per release — drop `UNIQUE (album_id)`, make `album_id` nullable with a plain index in its place, add `intake_item_id` (FK `intake_items`, `ON DELETE CASCADE`) and the CHECK `album_id IS NOT NULL OR intake_item_id IS NOT NULL` — plus `author` text with `author_user_id`, `recorded_by_user_id`, `medium`, the slip's fields (buzzwords, artist paragraph, review, recommended tracks, FCC line), `status` and `submitted_at`, the per-surface consent columns, and `credit`. Migration `0182` ([WXYC/Backend-Service#2873](https://github.com/WXYC/Backend-Service/pull/2873)) adds `review_revisions` (every submitted version of a review), `review_prints` (the print log: review, version, who, when) and `fcc_notes` (a note against a library release or an intake item, reported or confirmed), and adds `accepted_review_id`, `accepted_by` and `accepted_at` to `intake_items`. A later migration adds `rotation.moved_from_rotation_id`, the column the legacy-move part of the gate depends on. All Drizzle migrations under [`shared/database/src/migrations/`](../../shared/database/src/migrations/).
- Permission: the `reviews` station key (`read`, `write`, `manage`) is decided for all four roles in `WXYC_GRANTS` in [`shared/authentication/src/auth.roles.ts`](../../shared/authentication/src/auth.roles.ts). It is deliberately not `album_reviews`, which gates the Google Form archive ([ADR 0011](0011-album-review-submissions-separate-archive.md)).
- Per-row conditions on the intake transitions. The `reviews` key is the role-level grant. Two further conditions are per-row but are authorization, not state, and are checked separately from the row's effective state: only the DJ the item was requested for may accept or pass it, and only a holder of the `reviews:manage` grant, or the checkout's own holder (`checked_out_by`), may release it — a DJ cannot release someone else's checkout, and a checkout never releases itself. The identity condition is part of the UPDATE's WHERE, never a prior read — a read-then-authorize-then-UPDATE would let DJ A's release, authorized against A's own checkout, match DJ B's checkout of the same item taken in between. When no row matches, a single follow-up read decides: 403 when the item is in the right effective state but belongs to someone else, otherwise 409 `state_changed`. The state precondition proper is the effective-state condition itself: each transition is an `UPDATE … WHERE <effective-state precondition> [AND identity]` that returns 409 `state_changed` when no row matches — for example, two DJs racing to check out the same item, or one of these transitions attempted on an already-filed item (DELETE and PATCH on a filed item answer `already_filed`). A checkout whose holder's account has been deleted (`checked_out_by` null) can be released by a music director. A checkout of an expired request succeeds, since the effective-state condition treats an expired request as `pool`.
- Endpoints: `POST /intake`, `GET /intake`, `GET /intake/{id}`, `PATCH /intake/{id}`, `DELETE /intake/{id}`, `POST /intake/{id}/checkout`, `POST /intake/{id}/release`, `POST /intake/{id}/request`, `POST /intake/{id}/cancel-request`, `POST /intake/{id}/accept` (the requested DJ accepts the request), `POST /intake/{id}/pass`, `POST /intake/{id}/accept-review` (a music director accepts a review as the record's review; not `/accept`), `POST /intake/{id}/file`, `POST /intake/{id}/print`, `POST /intake/{id}/finalize`, the review routes under `/reviews` including `GET /reviews/{id}/revisions` (a review's edit history), `POST /library/{id}/print` (the slip for any typed, submitted review of a release already in the library), and the FCC note routes `POST /fcc-notes` (report), `GET /fcc-notes` (a record's notes and the music directors' waiting list), `POST /fcc-notes/{id}/confirm` and `DELETE /fcc-notes/{id}` (a music director may remove any note, and its reporter their own unconfirmed one). The FCC-report email to the music directors is sent by WXYC/Backend-Service#2863.
- The gate lives in the service layer, not in a route. A new code path that files a release or adds one to rotation goes through `insertAlbum` or `addToRotation`, and so inherits `REVIEW_GATE_CUTOVER_DATE`; an ESLint rule is what makes that "goes through" enforced rather than aspirational, catching a new raw `INSERT INTO library` that bypasses both functions. Catalog snapshot restore and the two legacy ETL jobs (`library-etl`, `rotation-etl`) write `library`/`rotation` rows outside the gate by design — they replay or import records that already existed, not new catalog entries — rather than being bypasses to close.
- Reviews are internal-only for v1. Consent flags are stored and nothing new is published with them. A listener-facing surface would be a later decision that reads the per-surface consent columns; it is not a flag on this table.
