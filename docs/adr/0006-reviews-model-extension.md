# 0006 — Reviews are many-per-release, scoped to an intake item or a library release, locked at print, consent-gated per surface

**Status:** Accepted, 2026-10-02. Mirrors the canonical cross-repo ADR 0005. This file supersedes the one-per-album, author-owned, MD-curated-queue model that the previous version of this ADR described; that model was superseded before any of it was built.

Canonical source: [`WXYC/wxyc-dj-ios/docs/cross-repo-adrs.md` ADR 0005](https://github.com/WXYC/wxyc-dj-ios/blob/main/docs/cross-repo-adrs.md#adr-0005--reviews-are-many-per-release-scoped-to-an-intake-item-or-a-library-release-locked-at-print-consent-gated-per-surface).

A review is about an _intake item_ (a physical copy the station holds, logged by a music director) or an existing library release, never a record the station does not hold. A release can have many reviews. Its content is what the station's printed slip carries: buzzwords, a paragraph about the artist, the review itself, recommended tracks, and FCC notes, all free text, plus the publishing consent described below. There is no rating, no headline, no per-track polarity, and no curated tag vocabulary.

Intake items wait in a pile. A music director logs each one into the pool, or requests a named DJ for it, which holds the record in the office for that DJ. A DJ claims an item by checking it out, which means physically taking the record; a requested DJ accepts (a checkout) or passes, and a pass notifies the music directors. A request lapses back to the pool after 7 days. A checkout never lapses, though after 14 days it is flagged overdue to the music directors. The holder or a music director can release a checked-out item back to the pool at any time. A music director can delete an item at any point before filing it, and its reviews are deleted with it.

Account holders write their own reviews. A DJ can start a review of an intake item only while holding it, because reviewing needs the physical record, and can review any release already in the library; trainees may review library releases but are steered to the pile. A music director records reviews on behalf of anyone else. The handwritten route is open to everyone: the review is written on paper and stays on the sleeve, and a music director records it in the app with all of its text optional and nothing printed. `author` is text with an optional account link (`author_user_id`), because most historical reviewers never had a Backend-Service account.

Drafts are private to their author and to whoever recorded them (`recorded_by_user_id`); music directors do not see other people's drafts. Submitting an intake review notifies the music directors, in the app and by email; submitting a review of a library release notifies nobody.

A review locks at print, not at submission. Only intake items are printed, one slip per record, which a music director prints and tapes to the album cover. The lock lives on the intake item, not on each review: until the item's slip is printed, an author edits their submitted review, and after it only music directors edit, and reprint. A review of a release already in the library is never printed and stays editable by its author indefinitely.

A review carries no routing: no outcome and no recommendation. The music director alone decides whether a release goes to rotation or straight to the shelf, and assigns its call number when filing it, before it is shelved; filing creates the library row, and the rotation entry if there is one, in one transaction. The librarian finalizes the release when shelving it, confirming or changing the call number.

After a cutover date, a hard gate applies: every release entering the library or rotation needs a submitted review, which includes a handwritten one recorded by a music director, or a citation. Releases catalogued before the cutover are deemed reviewed, since their physical reviews are already taped to their covers. A citation names either a release that has a submitted review or is deemed reviewed (for a second format or a replacement copy), or a form-archive submission dated before the form closed (including a record reviewed on the form before the cutover, which cites its own submission). A typed-text rotation record from before the cutover may still change bins after it, and keeps its right to be imported into the library by the librarian. The form and the app overlap through the rest of fall 2026; the cutover, and the form's close, is one day at the start of the spring 2027 semester. In this repository the gate is `REVIEW_GATE_CUTOVER_DATE`, enforced inside `insertAlbum` and `addToRotation`, so no caller can skip it.

Consent is collected per surface: one checkbox each for the website, the WXYC apps, and Instagram, plus one credit choice: DJ name (only if the account has one), real name, or no name. A review recorded on someone's behalf starts with nothing ticked. FCC notes are never published, on any surface. v1 collects this consent and publishes nothing new with it.

Author names may be shown anywhere inside the station: dj-site and the DJ apps. The form's anonymity promise covers only outside the station.

The form archive ([ADR 0011](0011-album-review-submissions-separate-archive.md)) stays a separate table; it is cited, never merged into `reviews`. Three of these decisions reach it: reviewer names may be shown inside the station; an intake item may cite an archive submission dated before the form closed; and a form-era "yes" to sharing counts for all three surfaces, always uncredited.

## Consequences

- Schema deltas: add `intake_items` and `intake_item_passes`; extend `reviews` to many per release, with `author` text plus `author_user_id`, `recorded_by_user_id`, `medium`, the slip's fields (buzzwords, artist paragraph, review, recommended tracks, FCC notes), `status`, the per-surface consent columns, and `credit`. All Drizzle migrations under [`shared/database/src/migrations/`](../../shared/database/src/migrations/).
- Permission: the `reviews` station key (`read`, `write`, `manage`) is decided for all four roles in `WXYC_GRANTS` in [`shared/authentication/src/auth.roles.ts`](../../shared/authentication/src/auth.roles.ts). It is deliberately not `album_reviews`, which gates the Google Form archive ([ADR 0011](0011-album-review-submissions-separate-archive.md)).
- Per-row conditions on the intake transitions. The `reviews` key is the role-level grant; these conditions are applied per row by the `/intake` routes, each as an `UPDATE … WHERE <effective-state precondition>` that returns 409 `state_changed` when no row matches. An accept or a pass is allowed only to the DJ the item was requested for, and only while the request has not expired. A release is allowed to a holder of the `reviews:manage` grant, or to the checkout's own holder (`checked_out_by`); a DJ cannot release someone else's checkout, and a checkout never releases itself. A checkout whose holder's account has been deleted (`checked_out_by` null) can be released by a music director. A checkout of an expired request succeeds, since an expired request is effectively `pool`.
- Endpoints (specified in the intake and review slices, not yet all shipped): `POST /intake/{id}/checkout`, `POST /intake/{id}/release`, `POST /intake/{id}/request`, `POST /intake/{id}/cancel-request`, `POST /intake/{id}/accept`, `POST /intake/{id}/pass`, and the review routes under `/reviews`.
- The gate lives in the service layer, not in a route. A new code path that files a release or adds one to rotation goes through `insertAlbum` or `addToRotation`, and so inherits `REVIEW_GATE_CUTOVER_DATE`.
- Reviews are internal-only for v1. Consent flags are stored and nothing new is published with them. A listener-facing surface would be a later decision that reads the per-surface consent columns; it is not a flag on this table.
