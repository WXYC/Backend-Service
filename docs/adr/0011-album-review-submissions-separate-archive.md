# 0011 — Form-sourced album review submissions are a separate archive, not the ADR 0006 Review model

WXYC's ~1,650 DJ-written album reviews collected since March 2021 through the "Album Review Responses" Google Form live in their own table, [`album_review_submissions`](../../shared/database/src/schema.ts) (migration 0119), synced nightly by [`jobs/album-reviews-etl/`](../../jobs/album-reviews-etl/) and read at `GET /album-reviews`. They do NOT extend the existing `reviews` table, which [ADR 0006](0006-reviews-model-extension.md) (canonical cross-repo ADR 0005 in the iOS DJ-tool repo) reserves as the one-per-album, author-owned (`author_dj_id` FK to `auth_user`), MD-queued in-app Review model with CRUD at `/reviews`.

The two review concepts conflict on every structural axis, which is why merging them was rejected (decision 2026-07-16, user-confirmed, after a first draft that evolved `reviews` was invalidated in review):

- **Cardinality**: the form archive holds multiple independent reviews per album (14 (artist, album) pairs have 2–3 today); ADR 0006's `reviews` is at most one per album (`album_id` UNIQUE).
- **Authorship**: form reviewers are free-text strings — many are alumni with no `auth_user` row and never will have one; ADR 0006 reviews are owned by a real account with edit/transfer rules.
- **Mutability**: form submissions are an append-only archive curated only via the sheet (the ETL UPSERTs content edits but never deletes and never reassigns identity); ADR 0006 reviews are living documents with author edits, MD transfer, and a claim queue.
- **Identity**: a submission's identity is free-text `(artist_name, album_title)` plus its `source_key`; `album_id` is a best-effort, never-overwritten singleton link that survives library deletions (FK ON DELETE SET NULL). ADR 0006 reviews are library-bound by construction.

## Decision

- `album_review_submissions` is the form archive: append-only, multi-per-album, free-text identity, PII-internal reviewer fields (`reviewer_raw`, `social_consent_raw`) that no read endpoint ever emits (the `flowsheet.dj_name` posture — the form promised "your name will not be shared").
- **There are two distinct read surfaces over this archive, with deliberately different consent postures. They must not be conflated, and neither may inherit the other's posture by copy-paste.**
  - **The consent-gated per-album attach — public/anonymous-reachable.** `GET /proxy/metadata/album` attaches a `wxycReviews` array (schema `WxycReviewItem`, wxyc-shared#259) for the album the request already resolved, served by `lookupWxycReviewsByAlbumId` in [`album-metadata-lookup.service.ts`](../../apps/backend/services/album-metadata-lookup.service.ts) and gated by the `WXYC_REVIEWS_ENABLED` env flag (default off). This is a **publishing** surface aimed at DJ- and listener-facing apps, so consent is a hard filter enforced in SQL — `social_consent = true`, never `IS NOT FALSE` and never `IS NOT NULL`, because `social_consent` is NULL whenever the ETL could not parse the reviewer's answer and an unparsed answer is not consent. The reviewer-identity columns are excluded by the query's explicit column list rather than by a downstream mapper.
  - **The role-gated internal browse — built (2026-08-26), the surface this ADR was amended for.** `GET /album-reviews` (with the resource-named `album_reviews` response array), served by [`album-reviews.service.ts`](../../apps/backend/services/album-reviews.service.ts) and gated by `requirePermissions({ reviews: ['read'] })` — held by `dj`, `musicDirector`, and `stationManager`, not by `member` and not by anonymous sessions, which carry no `role` claim at all. It returns **every row with a body**, with `social_consent` as a decorative field rather than a filter, and no env flag. Prototyped in the **closed** PR #1679 as an _anonymous-auth_ surface; that posture was rejected and the role gate is the difference. Its path was removed from `api.yaml` in the phantom-path sweep (wxyc-shared#378) while the `AlbumReview`/`AlbumReviewsResponse` schemas survived as orphans; re-adding the path is the follow-up that sweep's own "delete and re-add at the moment someone builds the thing" rule anticipated.
  - **Why the internal surface may serve non-consented rows, and why that is not a loophole.** The form's question, verbatim from the live sheet header, is: _"Are you comfortable with your review being shared on social media platforms? (AKA blog and/or Instagram, your name will not be shared)"_. Three things follow. It is **scoped to outside channels** — social media, glossed as blog and/or Instagram; internal station tools were never on the menu to decline. It is **not about anonymity** — the parenthetical promises name non-disclosure _unconditionally_, regardless of the answer, so a "no" is someone declining outside publication while already anonymous. And it is **a pick between two named channels**, which is what makes the lone `yes, only blog` answer legible. The measured split as of 2026-08-26, over rows with a body: **656 `true`, 32 `false`, 999 `NULL`**. The date ranges are cleanly disjoint at 2024-06-19/2024-06-21, so every NULL predates the question's own introduction (~2024-06-20): **those 999 reviewers were never asked**, which is invisible from the schema and is the fact that makes the internal posture defensible. Decision (user, 2026-08-26): a "no" here does not bar internal display.
  - **The cross-link that matters — a three-way model, not a two-way one.** Public/anonymous-reachable surfaces are consent-gated, always: `social_consent = true`, never `IS NOT FALSE`, never `IS NOT NULL`. Role-gated internal surfaces (dj and above) serve the full archive, for the scope reason above. Reviewer identity is excluded **everywhere, unconditionally** — on both surfaces, for consented and non-consented rows alike, by each query's explicit column list rather than by a downstream mapper. The rule this replaces said any revival of `/album-reviews` must adopt the attach's consent gate before reaching any client; that was written before the question's scope was read, and it forbade exactly this work. What survives from it is the part that was always right: **an anonymous-reachable client must never see this surface.** If `/album-reviews` is ever relaxed toward anonymous auth, or a new anonymous-reachable endpoint is pointed at this table, it inherits the attach's `social_consent = true` gate — no exceptions.
  - **A caller-varying field on the existing attach was considered and rejected.** `/proxy` applies `requirePermissions({})` at router level and `wxyc-ios-64` calls `/proxy/metadata/album` from 18 files on anonymous sessions, so a role-gated field cannot live there without changing the tier for every other proxy route. Worse, the response memo (`albumMetadataCacheKey`) is keyed on artist/release/track plus a flag-state hash — **not on the caller** — so a caller-varying field would serve the first DJ's payload to the next anonymous cache miss, publishing non-consented reviews to listeners. A separate endpoint avoids the class rather than managing it.
- The `/reviews` namespace, the `reviews` table, ADR 0006, and `tests/integration/fk-on-delete-flowsheet-rotation-reviews.spec.js` stay untouched and reserved for the in-app Review model.
- Neither model migrates into the other. A future in-app Review MAY cite a submission row as provenance (e.g. a `submission_id` reference added by the ADR 0006 implementation), but that is a citation, not a merge.

## Consequences

- Later work touching "reviews" must first decide which of the two concepts it means; this ADR is the boundary marker that prevents the archive from being folded into the in-app model (or vice versa) by well-meaning consolidation.
- **Consent is a row-level property enforced in SQL, never a deployment-level one.** `WXYC_REVIEWS_ENABLED` decides whether the surface is served at all; `social_consent = true` decides which rows may appear on it. Flipping the flag on can only widen the audience for rows that already cleared consent — it can never promote a non-consented row. Any future read path over this table inherits that split: the flag is optional, the SQL predicate is not.
- **The gate is conservative about ambiguous answers, not about qualified ones — and that is its real limit.** `normalizeSocialConsent` (`jobs/album-reviews-etl/map.ts`) maps any answer starting with `yes`, plus `ya` and `ok*`, to `true`; blank and unrecognized text fall to NULL. That closed vocabulary is deliberately strict at the ambiguous end, which is what makes `= true` the right predicate. It is deliberately _loose_ at the other end: the variant it was written for is "yes, but take my name off", and names are never emitted regardless. What it cannot distinguish is a **conditional** affirmative — "yes, but cut the last paragraph", "yes if you drop the bit about the label" — from an unconditional one, while the attach serves the **full unedited body**. No SQL predicate can close that gap, because the qualification lives in prose in `social_consent_raw`. The mitigation is a one-time human read of `social_consent_raw` across the servable cohort before `WXYC_REVIEWS_ENABLED` is first flipped on, and it belongs in the light-up procedure (`docs/env-vars.md`) rather than in code.
- **The two surfaces must be tested in different tiers, and the gate has exactly one CI-enforced guard.** The integration suite (`tests/integration/album-reviews.spec.js`) runs `AUTH_BYPASS=true`, under which `requirePermissions` returns `next()` before the permission block is reached — so it can pin row/consent/PII behavior but is structurally blind to which roles are admitted, and `npm run ci:testmock` proves nothing about the gate (its testMatch is integration-only besides). The e2e suite is the only tier with `AUTH_BYPASS=false`, but no workflow invokes `jest.e2e.config.json`, so it is a pre-push gate rather than a CI guard. The guard that actually runs in CI is `tests/unit/routes/album-reviews-permissions.route.test.ts`, which mocks `jose`, wires the real middleware back over the shared `@wxyc/authentication` mock, and injects a role. Do not delete it, and do not "simplify" it by extending `tests/mocks/authentication.mock.ts` — that mock ignores its permission argument by design and is mapped into every unit test in the repo.
- **`stationManager` does not inherit the grant — it is decided, like every other role.** `reviews` is declared in `stationStatement` and decided for all four roles in `WXYC_GRANTS` (`read` for dj/musicDirector/stationManager, an explicit `[]` denial for member), because that matrix is typed-total: omitting a role is a compile error. `requirePermissions` remains a pure per-role check with no hierarchy, so nothing about being the top role confers this grant. Before the matrix landed, `stationManager` was built as `{ ...adminAc.statements, ... }` — a spread that confers better-auth's org-plugin resources and no custom key, while reading like a wildcard — and a key granted to dj but trusted to arrive there produced exactly the inversion where the most privileged account 403s and a plain DJ gets 200. This grant is the first new statement key since, and the case the matrix was built for.
- **The archive's reach through the consented attach is a small fraction of the archive.** Three gates compose — consented, linked to a `library` album, and non-empty body — and as of 2026-08-25 they leave 252 of 1,689 rows (744 linked; 656 consented). A reader comparing "1,650 reviews" against what the app shows should expect that gap and not read it as a bug. Raising the _linked_ share is `jobs/album-reviews-etl/`'s concern, not the serve path's.
- **The attach mirrors the external critic-reviews mechanism (ADR 0012) structurally but not editorially.** Critic reviews are excerpt-capped with a mandatory link-out because they are third-party copyrighted text; DJ reviews are WXYC-authored, so the full body is surfaced. The binding constraint here is consent and reviewer anonymity, not copyright — which is why the two keep separate flags, separate tables, and separate wire fields.
- **Reaching the listener app's playcut-detail view would need a contract change, and is not what the attach above does.** `GET /proxy/metadata/album` is called unconditionally by the DJ-facing catalog surfaces (`wxyc-dj-ios`, `wxyc-dj-android`, dj-site), but `wxyc-ios-64` skips it for rows whose `metadata_status` is terminal — the short-circuit that made external critic reviews unreachable there until [BS#1870](https://github.com/WXYC/Backend-Service/issues/1870) / [#1872](https://github.com/WXYC/Backend-Service/issues/1872), under epic [BS#1869](https://github.com/WXYC/Backend-Service/issues/1869), inlined `critic_reviews` into the V2 flowsheet feed. Surfacing DJ reviews on that same playcut path would likewise require a new `wxyc_reviews` field on `FlowsheetV2TrackEntry` in wxyc-shared plus a feed-assembly attach; that is a separate, deliberate decision, not an automatic mirror of #1869.
- The archive's writer is exactly one job (`jobs/album-reviews-etl/`); its link pass is the only writer of `album_review_submissions.album_id`, and manual corrections always win over it. **`album_id` is the only column that holds.** Every content column — `review`, `social_consent` and the rest of `SET_CONTENT_COLUMNS` in `jobs/album-reviews-etl/writer.ts` — is re-asserted from the Google Sheet by each nightly UPSERT, so this archive has no durable in-database suppression mechanism: a hand-written `UPDATE` that revokes a row's consent or blanks its body is silently reverted within 24 hours. Curation of a servable row has to happen in the response sheet, upstream of the ETL. Any future need to withhold a row _at the database_ would require a new column outside the ON CONFLICT set, and should be designed as one rather than improvised as a direct edit.
- **Withdrawing consent is not immediate, and the tail is roughly a day.** Two caches sit between the response sheet and the wire, and they compose: the ETL runs nightly (`50 4 * * *` UTC), so a sheet edit takes up to ~24h to reach `social_consent` in Postgres; and `GET /proxy/metadata/album` memoizes the assembled response — `wxycReviews` included, since `extractAlbumMetadataEnrichment` excludes only the three request-echoed base fields — for 1h. Worst case, a review stays served for about 25 hours after a reviewer asks for it to come down. That is acceptable for a slow-moving archive and is stated here so nobody has to rediscover it under pressure, but it is the number to quote if the question is ever asked, and it is why an urgent takedown is a flag flip (`WXYC_REVIEWS_ENABLED=false`, container recreate — which clears the memo) rather than a data edit.
- The dormant `feature/library-code-lookup-reviews` branch (PR #212, closed stale 2026-05-09) targets ADR 0006's `reviews` and is unaffected.
